## Why patients fly to China for implants

Dental implants are the most common reason overseas patients contact us. A single implant that costs **$3,000–$6,000 in the United States** is often **$1,100–$2,800 in China** — using the same international implant brands (Straumann, Nobel Biocare, Osstem) that Western clinics use.

This guide explains what you actually pay for, the real price ranges in 2026, and the seven costs people forget to ask about before they book a flight.

## What is included in an implant price?

A modern dental implant is not one object — it is a small system:

- **The implant fixture** — the titanium screw placed in the bone.
- **The abutment** — the connector between fixture and crown.
- **The crown** — the visible tooth on top.

In China, a quoted "single implant" price usually includes all three. Always confirm this in writing, because in some countries the crown is quoted separately.

> Typical savings on the implant procedure alone are **60–75%** versus US prices. Add flights and accommodation, and most full-arch plans still save thousands.

## What drives the final price

Five factors change your quote more than anything else:

1. **Implant brand** — premium Swiss/German brands cost more than Korean brands, but both are excellent.
2. **Bone condition** — if you have bone loss, a graft or sinus lift is added.
3. **Number of teeth** — full-arch solutions (All-on-4) are priced per arch, not per tooth.
4. **City** — Beijing and top Guizhou clinics sit at the higher end; smaller cities are cheaper.
5. **Temporary teeth** — ask whether a temporary crown or denture is included in the quote.

## The seven costs people forget

None of these are "tricks" — they are mostly differences in how quotes are written. Asking in advance avoids surprises:

- Is the **crown** included in the implant price?
- Are **abutment** fees separate?
- Will you need a **bone graft or sinus lift** (unknown before a CBCT scan)?
- Are **pre-op extraction and gum treatment** billed separately?
- Is a **temporary denture** included in full-arch plans?
- How many **check-ups and adjustments** are free afterwards?
- Are **imaging fees** (CBCT, panoramic X-ray) line items on their own?

## Does the trip still save money?

For a single implant, flights and hotels can erase the saving. For **All-on-4 or multiple implants**, the math almost always works — the treatment saving usually beats travel costs several times over.

If you were already planning to visit China, flights are not an extra cost. As long as the treatment saving beats your stay, the trip is worthwhile. We calculate this for every patient and tell you honestly when a trip is **not** worth it.
